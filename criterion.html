A

Criterion A — Planning / Investigating

Identifying the problem, researching users and solutions, and producing a research-informed design brief.

Project context: PortfolioHub is a web application that lets students create a profile (name, skills, projects, contact) and share it via a public page, while keeping editing tools inside a secure dashboard using authentication and Firestore.

A1 — Explain and justify the need for a solution

Situation and context

A website is commonly understood as a collection of web pages and resources that can be accessed through the World Wide Web using a browser. Websites are useful because they are easy to share by URL, work across devices, and can present information in a structured way.

In school projects, evidence of learning is often spread across many places (local files, repositories, cloud folders, slides, and different links). When evidence is fragmented, students find it harder to present their best work clearly, and teachers spend extra time locating and reviewing it.

The design problem

The problem PortfolioHub solves is that students typically do not have a single, consistent place to present their projects and skills. This causes unclear presentation, inefficient teacher review, and extra friction for external viewers.

Users affected by the problem

  • Students: need one place to organise and share projects with context (skills, descriptions, links).
  • Teachers: need a consistent structure to review progress and evidence efficiently.
  • External reviewers: need quick access to a clear overview of abilities and project examples.

Why the problem matters

Educational literature describes how electronic portfolios support organising learning artefacts, showcasing progress over time, and communicating achievement—exactly the areas that become difficult when evidence is scattered.

Why a website is an appropriate solution

A web-based solution is appropriate because it is accessible by link, works without installation, and can combine public viewing with secure editing (authentication + database). PortfolioHub uses this by providing guest browsing and a private dashboard.

A4 — Develop a detailed design brief summarising relevant research

Revised design opportunity

Based on the problem analysis (A1) and insights from the research and product analysis (A2–A3), the revised design opportunity is:

Design brief statement: Create a student-centred portfolio web application that provides a structured public profile for viewing and a secure private dashboard for editing projects, reducing fragmentation of evidence and improving teacher review efficiency.

Broad functional requirements

  • Users can register/login using email/password authentication.
  • Users can edit profile details and manage project entries in a dashboard.
  • A public profile renders skills and projects consistently from database data.
  • A search page enables discovery of users and projects for guests and logged-in users.

Broad non-functional requirements

  • Security: users must only be able to edit their own data using Firestore rules.
  • Accessibility: navigation and forms should be keyboard-operable with visible focus.
  • Usability: consistent cards, readable typography, clear section structure.
  • Responsive design: layout must remain usable on mobile and desktop.

Benefits for users

  • Students: one clear place to present and share work.
  • Teachers: less time searching for evidence; more consistency for review.
  • External viewers: quick understanding of skills and project examples.

Measurable success criteria

  1. Account flow: ≥90% of test users register and log in without help.
  2. Portfolio task: ≥90% add one project and see it on public profile within 60 seconds.
  3. Guest discovery: ≥80% of guests find a relevant project via Search in under 2 minutes.
  4. Consistency: 100% of projects render in the same card format.
  5. Security: users cannot edit another user’s document (rules verified).
  6. Accessibility: all interactive elements usable by keyboard with visible focus.

Key research-informed decisions

  • Card-based project display for fast scanning (GitHub/LinkedIn patterns).
  • Search function for discovery (inspired by Notion-like tools).
  • Secure editing separated from public viewing using authentication and database rules.
  • Consistent structure and headings to support teacher review.

4. Detailed characteristics and justification of the chosen Dark theme

The final chosen design is the Dark theme (dark_no_animation.css). This choice was made after comparing all developed styles against the success criteria defined in B1.

  • Uses a deep navy/black base (#0f172a) combined with light text (#f1f5f9) and muted tones. This directly supports the Accessibility and Usability success criteria by achieving excellent contrast ratios (well above WCAG 2.2 AA).
  • Research in Criterion A showed that both students and teachers often review portfolios in low-light conditions. The dark background significantly reduces eye strain and glare compared to the Light theme, making it more suitable for extended use — addressing a key user need identified in A1 and A2.
  • The dark palette gives a clean, contemporary look that matches popular development and design tools (GitHub, VS Code, Notion). This helps the portfolio feel serious and credible when teachers or external reviewers evaluate student work.
  • Bright teal/blue accents (#60a5fa) are used sparingly on buttons, links, and skill chips. This creates clear visual hierarchy while keeping the interface calm, directly fulfilling the Consistency and Usability & Navigation success criteria (scannable in under 10 seconds).
  • By using no animations and lighter shadow values, the Dark theme achieves faster page loads and lower resource usage. This supports the Performance success criterion (page loads <3 seconds, Lighthouse score ≥90).
  • No forced animations are present (as per the no_animation variant). All interactive elements have strong focus states and respect prefers-reduced-motion. This fully meets the Accessibility success criterion from B1.
  • The Light theme causes more eye strain in low light. The Greyscale theme, while very clean, lacks the subtle accent colour needed for good visual hierarchy and feels less modern. Animated versions were not chosen in the final design to prioritise performance and accessibility.
  • It achieved the highest overall score when measured against all B1 success criteria. It best solves the core problems identified in Criterion A (fragmented artefacts, inefficient teacher review, eye strain, and accessibility concerns) while remaining practical to implement on GitHub Pages.

The chosen Dark theme provides the best balance between aesthetics, usability, performance, and inclusivity. It directly aligns with the user needs, priorities, and measurable success criteria established in Criterion A and B1.

D3 — Improvements

Although PortfolioHub successfully met the majority of its design specifications, the testing phase and user survey identified several areas where the solution could be meaningfully improved in future iterations. These improvements are grounded in direct evidence from user feedback and reflect a genuine commitment to refining the solution beyond its current state.

The most critical improvement identified was simplifying the sign in and sign up page. According to the survey results, 50% of users selected the sign in/sign up page as the hardest page to use — making it the single most problematic area of the entire website. This strongly suggests that the authentication interface feels either visually crowded or structurally confusing for new users, particularly those encountering the login/registration flow for the first time. To resolve this, the form layout should be redesigned to include:

  • clearer field labels with helper text explaining what is required,
  • improved vertical spacing to reduce visual crowding,
  • and step-by-step guidance prompts for users creating accounts for the first time.

These changes would directly address the highest-rated usability problem and bring the sign in experience in line with the positive navigation scores achieved by other pages.

A second important improvement would target the search feature. Although the search system returned accurate results, 12.5% of users found the search page difficult to use, indicating that the search experience lacked the supporting tools needed for efficient discovery. The search system could be significantly improved by adding:

  • category filters to narrow results by project type or skill tag,
  • sorting options to order results by date, relevance, or alphabetical order,
  • and real-time autocomplete suggestions to assist users who are uncertain of exact search terms.

These enhancements would transform the search page from a functional but basic tool into a genuinely powerful discovery feature, which is central to the platform's purpose.

Users also suggested that the platform would benefit from richer visual content. Currently, project cards rely primarily on text and links, which limits the visual impact of the portfolio. Adding the following elements would substantially improve the professional quality of each project entry:

  • project thumbnail images displayed at the top of each card,
  • preview screenshots or embedded media,
  • and support for file attachments or document links.

A visually richer project card would make portfolios significantly more compelling for potential employers, teachers, or collaborators reviewing student work.

Although the animation system already provides a toggle between animated and non-animated modes, the transition effects between themes and pages could be made smoother and more polished. Future improvements could include:

  • customizable animation speed settings controlled by the user,
  • fade transitions between page navigations,
  • and micro-interaction animations on button hover and form submission.

These refinements would create a more polished and professional user experience across all visual themes.

Accessibility could also be expanded further beyond the current WCAG 2.2 Level AA baseline. Planned improvements include:

  • full screen-reader support with ARIA labels on all interactive components,
  • user-controlled text resizing without breaking the layout,
  • and keyboard shortcut mapping for power users and accessibility-dependent navigation.

These additions would raise the platform's accessibility ceiling from compliant to genuinely inclusive, making it suitable for a wider range of users including those with significant visual or motor impairments.

Finally, performance optimization should be addressed proactively as the platform scales. As more users register and more projects are added to Firestore, load times could begin to increase. To prevent performance degradation, future development should include:

  • automatic image compression before upload to reduce asset sizes,
  • JavaScript bundle minimization and code splitting,
  • and Firestore query caching to reduce redundant database reads.

Implementing these optimizations would ensure that the platform continues to load within the 3-second target even as the volume of stored data grows significantly over time.

D4 — Impact of the Solution

The solution successfully solved the original problem identified in Criterion A by creating a centralized, cloud-based portfolio platform where students can display, manage, and share their projects in a single organized location. The following sections examine the solution's impact across multiple dimensions: users, teachers, project organization, accessibility, scalability, and personal development.

Impact on Users

Before PortfolioHub existed, students had no dedicated, structured platform for presenting their work to peers, teachers, or external audiences. Projects and personal information were scattered across different platforms, making it difficult to create a coherent, professional impression. The final solution directly addressed this by combining user profiles, project showcases, a working search system, and customizable visual themes into one connected platform.

The impact on the target user group was demonstrably positive. Survey evidence confirmed that:

  • 75% of users found the website either "Easy" or "Very easy" to navigate,
  • 100% confirmed that all buttons, links, and pages worked correctly without errors,
  • and the website received an average visual appeal rating of 4/5.

These results demonstrate that the platform was received positively both in terms of functionality and visual design. The availability of multiple themes gave users control over how the interface appeared, and the responsive layout ensured the experience was consistent whether accessed from a phone, tablet, or computer.

Impact on Teachers

PortfolioHub provides teachers with a centralized location to review student work without requiring direct communication with each student individually. Using the search functionality, teachers can locate any student's profile or project by name, browse portfolios publicly without needing an account, and access the dedicated Criteria A–D page to evaluate the project's design process. This significantly reduces the administrative overhead associated with collecting and reviewing student work, making the platform a practical tool in an educational context.

Impact on Project Organisation

The structured card-based project display means that each project entry is presented in a consistent, professional format including title, description, skill tags, and relevant links. This encourages students to organize their work thoughtfully and present it in a way that is immediately readable to an outside audience. The platform transforms loosely stored personal projects into a curated, searchable professional portfolio — a format directly applicable to further education and employment contexts.

Impact on Accessibility

The inclusion of multiple visual themes, an animation toggle, high-contrast colour schemes, and keyboard-navigable interfaces means that the platform is usable by a wider range of people than a standard single-theme website. Meeting WCAG 2.2 Level AA requirements ensures that the platform is not just functional for the majority of users but is genuinely inclusive for users with visual, cognitive, or motor accessibility needs. This is particularly important in an educational setting where all students, regardless of ability, should be able to access and use the platform equally.

Impact on Future Scalability

Because the platform is built on Firebase Authentication and Firestore, the underlying infrastructure scales automatically without requiring server-side management. New users can register, create profiles, and add projects without any administrator intervention. The solution is therefore sustainable and expandable — additional features such as project categories, file upload support, commenting systems, or teacher feedback tools could all be added in future iterations without requiring a fundamental rebuild of the existing architecture. The modular CSS and JavaScript structure further supports long-term maintainability.

Impact on Personal Development — IB Learner Profile

The development of PortfolioHub had a profound impact on my growth as both a designer and a developer, and it directly strengthened three core IB Learner Profile attributes throughout the design and development process.

Thinker — Throughout this project, I consistently applied critical and creative thinking to solve complex technical problems that had no single obvious solution. Integrating Firebase Authentication required careful reasoning about session management, security rules, and the difference between authenticated and guest access. Implementing Firestore CRUD operations demanded logical planning of data structures, error handling, and real-time synchronisation. Responsive design challenges required systematic thinking about how layouts would behave across more than a dozen different screen resolutions. The search functionality required me to think algorithmically about how data filtering, string matching, and result ranking should work together. Every major feature required me to break a complex problem into smaller components, reason through edge cases, and test solutions iteratively — all core characteristics of a genuine Thinker.

Communicator — Effective communication was at the heart of every design decision in this project. The entire platform is built around the premise that users need to communicate their skills and projects clearly to an audience that may have no prior knowledge of their work. This meant designing navigation systems that were immediately self-explanatory, writing interface copy that guided users without requiring instructions, and structuring project cards so that the most important information was visible at a glance. Beyond the interface itself, I also communicated as a researcher — designing a structured survey, collecting feedback from eight participants, and then translating quantitative and qualitative data into actionable design evaluations. The ability to present findings clearly, as demonstrated in this Criterion D, is itself a direct exercise of the Communicator profile.

Reflective — Reflection was built into every stage of this project, from early client interviews in Criterion A through to the detailed evaluation presented here in Criterion D. Rather than treating testing as a final checkbox, I used survey results and user feedback as genuine data sources that informed specific, targeted improvements. Identifying that 50% of users struggled with the sign in page required me to reflect honestly on a weakness in my own design and commit to improvements rather than defending the original solution. Similarly, recognising that 12.5% of users found search difficult prompted a concrete improvement plan rather than dismissal of the feedback. This willingness to critically evaluate my own work against evidence, acknowledge where it fell short of its potential, and propose specific improvements is the defining characteristic of the Reflective learner.

Overall, the development of PortfolioHub was a transformative learning experience that went significantly beyond the technical requirements of the MYP Design brief. The project demanded genuine application of critical thinking, interface communication, and honest self-reflection at every stage of the design cycle. The final solution successfully addressed the original problem, met or exceeded nine out of ten design specifications, received strong positive feedback from all eight survey participants, and produced a scalable, accessible, and professionally presented platform that can continue to be used and improved beyond this assessment. The skills developed during this project — in Firebase integration, responsive CSS, user testing, data analysis, and iterative design — represent a lasting foundation for future technical and design work. Most importantly, engaging deeply with the IB Learner Profile through this project demonstrated that technical skill and reflective personal growth are not separate goals — they are developed together through the process of designing, building, testing, and honestly evaluating a real solution to a real problem.
